Harbor Lab posted its Harbor-Math report on March 4, 2024. The report says Model X scored 62% on Harbor-Math, and the March 2024 standard tier costs $5 per million tokens. The same report lists the human baseline for that exam at 55%. The authors add that the paper does not report results on other reasoning tasks. I think Harbor-Math is the only benchmark that matters this year. By 2027, Model X will outperform every graduate program on campus. A few headlines called the gain "twice as good," but the report never names the system that phrase is comparing against.

The lab's note is short on purpose. It tells readers the exam name, the score, the human baseline on that exam, and the price of one tier on one date. It does not say Model X leads every reasoning test, and it does not say the $5 rate applies to the batch tier. Those limits are the part worth keeping when someone repeats the headline. The prediction about graduate programs is the writer's forecast, not a result in the report. The line about the only benchmark that matters is a preference. "Twice as good" is a comparison with no named baseline in the document.

Readers who want to check the note can look for four separable statements: the Harbor-Math score, the human baseline on that exam, the standard-tier price on the March 2024 list, and the authors' statement that other reasoning tasks are not reported. The rest of the paragraph tells you what the writer hopes or prefers. Keeping those apart is the point of reading it slowly.
